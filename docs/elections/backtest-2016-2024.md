# Backtest: 2016–2024 baselines (methodology doc §43)

Rebuilt 2026-08-23 under methodology **2026.09.6**, which additionally
discounts stateCooperation by the administering institution's structural
insulation — a statutorily balanced election board (NY, WI, IL, HI) halves
the dimension, a partisan-majority board (NC, VA, MD, OK, SC) takes a
quarter off, an elected or governor-appointed official carries no discount
(`packages/elections-shared/src/administration.ts`), and never classifies
separate opinions as rulings (a docketed dissent quoting the injunction
order it dissents from had counted as a second blocking injunction). The
same build loads the historical state-legislation layer into every
cycle. The 2026-08-22 rebuild
(after the critical review, `critical-review-2026-08-22.md`, found
look-ahead bias in the original run) compared cycles without state
legislation — the live OpenStates source cannot time-travel — and used a
matched-sources construct for cross-cycle claims. That gap is closed: the
OpenStates bulk archive (monthly Postgres dump) carries dated action
histories back past 2015, and `legislation:fetch`'s six quoted-phrase
queries were replayed against it (`scripts/elections/extract-history.sql`),
yielding a
13,517-bill cache that `legislation:load` applied to each cycle's window
with point-in-time status. Cross-cycle comparisons are now full-vs-full;
matched-sources survives as a sensitivity note. This report replaces the
earlier findings; superseded numbers are kept at the bottom for the record.

## What changed and why the old headline was wrong

The original backtest read every prior cycle at zero high-risk pivotal races
and concluded 2026 was anomalous. That result was an artifact: replays
judged every event and case by its **current** lifecycle status, so a docket
that terminated in December contributed nothing on the August days it was
live. By election-day vantage, 93% of 2020's cases were terminated — so the
2020 replay saw almost no live litigation on any day. The live 2026 cycle,
whose cases have not had time to die, was being compared against baselines
with the litigation stripped out.

2026.09.4 records status-transition dates (docket termination, rule
supersession) and resolves each event's and case's status **as of the
replay day**. Residual unknown-transition rows are under 3% of cases per
cycle (0–1 events). The same rebuild applied the ruling-classifier fixes
(stays of injunctions no longer count as injunctions, filing-mechanics
orders are excluded, federal *plaintiffs* no longer nationalize a block,
identical orders on transferred dockets dedupe) — across the five baselines
that removed 74 misclassified entries and 48 duplicates.

**The finding, stated carefully:** with identical sources on both sides —
state legislation now included in every cycle — 2026 reads 140 at 72 days
out, level with 2024 at the same distance (141), above 2022 (132) and 2020
(127), inside the recent-cycle range. 2024 finished at 149. **2026 is not
anomalous.** It sits at the top of a range that litigious ordinary cycles
also occupy, and the model's reading is "as much live, pivotal-seat
election activity as 2024 at this point," not a verdict that subversion is
underway. (Sensitivity: with the state-legislation layer removed from the
live model, 2026 reads 81 — the ordering against prior cycles' matched
numbers is unchanged.)

## How to read each cycle

- **2016 / 2018 — zero throughout, but not from quiet dockets.** Both
  cycles carry saturated litigation exposure (.83/.82). The zeros come from
  the pivotality term: the margin-derived projections give 244R and 245R —
  blowout majorities — and national tightness clamps to 0, so no race is
  pivotal. This is the multiplicative design working as intended (a
  non-tight House can't flip on a handful of seats), not the model
  discriminating subversion risk. RECAP coverage is thinner in these
  cycles, and the legislative record is too: the OpenStates archive holds
  ~16–22k bills/year for 2015–16 against ~100k+ later (138 window bills
  loaded for 2016 vs 1,106+ for every later cycle). All three effects
  point the same way.
- **2020 — high and declining.** 127 at 72 days out, decaying to 92 by
  election day as courts resolved cases (institutional resistance .67 —
  the "courts held" signal survives the correction, now in proportion).
  Note: 2020's projection allocates 434 of 435 seats, so tightness uses
  the 0.5 fallback.
- **2022 — high throughout** (126–139, ending 129). Redistricting-cycle
  litigation, the heaviest legislative volume of any cycle (1,902 window
  bills), and a genuinely tight projected House. 8 races lack a blended
  margin, so tightness also uses the 0.5 fallback.
- **2024 — the tightest cycle** (projection 218–217, tightness 1.0) with
  heavy litigation: 137 rising to 149 at election day.
- **2026 — 140 full model at 72 days out**, tightness .76. The
  administration-structure discount reorders the race-level top of the
  list (New Jersey and Arizona toss-ups now lead; New York's drop to
  ~half stateCooperation moves its toss-ups from #1–2 to #5–7) while
  barely moving the aggregate count — most high-risk races clear the .12
  cutoff with or without the discount. Sensitivity:
  81 with the state-legislation layer marked immaterial
  (`elections_sens_2026`) — retained as a robustness check now that the
  layer exists in every cycle, no longer needed for comparability.

## Consequences

1. **The threat scale is calibrated to baseline percentiles** (owner
   decision, 2026-08-22). `controlThreat` is the live count's position
   in the pooled distribution of all 505 baseline cycle-days
   (`packages/elections-shared/src/threat-calibration.ts`, regenerated by
   `baseline:export`): LOW below the median, MODERATE from the median,
   HIGH at or above the 90th percentile. With legislation in the
   baselines the pooled distribution shifted up — cutpoints are now
   p50 = 115 and p90 = 145 (previously 51/84) — and the live full-model
   count (140) reads **MODERATE**, just under the HIGH cutpoint. The
   earlier HIGH reading was an artifact of comparing a legislation-
   inclusive live count against legislation-blind baselines; that
   asymmetry, and the conservative-lean caveat it required, are gone.
2. **The high-risk cutoff (.12) sits inside a dense part of the
   distribution** — day-to-day counts move by tens of races. Counts are
   best read as a band, not a point.

## Standing limitations

- Injunction-as-resistance is direction-blind: courts blocking expansions
  and restrictions both count as resistance (documented; visible in 2020).
- State-court and county incidents (Cochise-class) are under-covered in
  every cycle.
- The legislative record thins before 2017: the OpenStates archive holds
  ~16–22k bills/year for 2015–16 vs ~100k+ later, so 2016's
  state-legislation layer is structurally undercounted (138 window bills).
- The matched-source 2026 run (`elections_sens_2026`) is retained as a
  sensitivity check only; comparisons no longer depend on it.
- Administration structures are held constant across the window; the two
  mid-window changes (Wisconsin's commission created 2016, North
  Carolina's appointing authority moved 2025) are not modeled.
- Members/incumbent display fields in baselines come from current-day
  congress-legislators; assessments are unaffected.

## Reproducing

```sh
PGURL=postgresql://…  RATINGS_FILE=/path/to/1976-2024-house.tab \
  scripts/elections/build-baseline.sh 2020        # any of 2016–2024
pnpm --filter @elections-tracker/worker reclassify:rulings   # per DB
# Historical legislation: restore the OpenStates monthly pgdump
# (https://data.openstates.org/postgres/monthly/YYYY-MM-public.pgdump plus
# its schema file), run scripts/elections/extract-history.sql against it to
# regenerate
# data/openstates-history.jsonl, then:
scripts/elections/load-legislation.sh   # loads + replays + compare/export
```

## Superseded results (2026.09.3, look-ahead biased — kept for the record)

The original run read: 2016–2022 zero for the entire final 100 days; 2024
zero until ~48 hours out (then 3); 2026 matched-sources 4. Its "no prior
cycle comes close" conclusion, and the "record activity met record
resistance and read zero" reading of 2020, were artifacts of judging
historical days by current case status.
