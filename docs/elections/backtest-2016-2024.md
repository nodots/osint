# Backtest: 2016–2024 baselines (methodology doc §43)

*Extended 2026-08-22 to five cycles. Headline: 2016, 2018, 2020, and 2022
all read ZERO high-risk pivotal races for their entire final 100 days; 2024
alone touched 3 in its final ~48 hours. 2026 reads 4 on matched sources at
73+ days out.*

**The 2020 result deserves its own sentence.** 2020 had the largest source
volumes of any cycle — 423 voting dockets, 225 ruling entries, 101 blocking
injunctions — and still read zero, because litigation exposure (.73, the
highest input recorded) was met with institutional resistance of .99. The
model's reading of 2020 is "record activity, courts held," which matches the
historical consensus. Caveat: the injunction-as-resistance heuristic is
direction-blind — 2020's courts blocked both restrictions and expansions of
voting access, and both count as resistance here. The certification crisis
was post-election and presidential-centric, outside this pre-election
House-scoped window.

| final-100-day max | 2016 | 2018 | 2020 | 2022 | 2024 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| high-risk pivotal races | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 3 |
| dockets / blocking injunctions | 284/29 | 221/28 | 423/101 | 334/41 | 298/31 |
| litigation / pressure / resistance (end) | 50/28/87 | 56/39/63 | 73/39/99 | 44/38/89 | 72/45/90 |

RECAP coverage thins before ~2018 — treat 2016's volumes as a floor.

*(Original two-cycle report follows; its findings are unchanged.)*


Built 2026-08-22 with `scripts/elections/build-baseline.sh` under methodology
2026.09.3. Each baseline replays the identical pipeline against a past House
cycle: that cycle's districts (Census CD118), candidates and incumbency (FEC
`cn22`/`cn24`), derived ratings from the two prior cycles' MEDSL margins, the
era's events from the same sources windowed by `INGEST_FROM`/`INGEST_UNTIL`,
and a 100-day assessment replay ending on that election day. Every replayed
row is stamped `retrodiction: true`.

## Headline series — high-risk pivotal races (the count the threat level is defined on)

| | 2022 | 2024 | 2026 (as of 73 days out) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Final 100 days, minimum | 0 | 0 | 12 |
| Final 100 days, maximum | 0 | 3 | 19 |
| At election day / today | 0 | 3 | 17 |
| First nonzero | never | ~2 days before election day | before the observed window |
| Threat level | LOW throughout | LOW until the final days, then HIGH | HIGH throughout |

**The false-positive test passes.** Ordinary cycles read zero high-risk
pivotal races essentially the whole run-up: 2022 never left zero; 2024
touched 3 only in its final ~48 hours (consistent with the late-October 2024
litigation flurry). The model does not rate ordinary close elections as
dangerous.

## The source-parity check (the important caveat)

The 2026 model ingests state election legislation (OpenStates), which cannot
be replayed historically — its API filters by record-update time. Both
baselines therefore have `stateCooperation = 0` structurally. A sensitivity
run of 2026 with OpenStates events excluded gives the like-for-like
comparison:

| Matched sources, matched ~distance from election day | 2022 | 2024 | 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| High-risk pivotal races | 0 | 0 | **4** |
| Mean process vulnerability | .127 | .146 | .163 |

So the finding, stated carefully:

1. **On identical sources at the same point in the cycle, 2026 reads 4 —
   a state neither baseline reached at any point before its final week.**
2. The full 2026 model reads 17; the difference (4 → 17) is the state
   legislation layer, which is real signal but has no historical
   counterpart. Cross-cycle comparisons must use the matched-source number.
3. Composition differs beyond the counts: 2026 shows higher litigation
   exposure (.86 vs .72/.44), higher active pressure (.59 vs .45/.38), and
   less resistance headroom (.82 vs .89/.90) than either baseline's final
   state.

## Event-volume parity (density-bias check)

| material events | 2022 | 2024 | 2026 (to date) |
|---|---|---|---|
| CourtListener dockets | 334 | 298 | 233 |
| CourtListener rulings | 95 | 100 | 45 |
| — of which blocking injunctions | 41 | 31 | 16 |
| Federal Register | 18 | 19 | 17 |
| SCOTUS opinions | 3 | 0 | 3 |

Raw volumes are comparable — past cycles actually produced *more* voting
litigation in absolute terms — so 2026's elevation is compositional
(national scope, federal-action pattern, pressure recency), not an artifact
of growing source coverage. RECAP's coverage does grow over time; with
volumes in the same range across cycles, that bias is bounded here.

## Other limitations

- Members/incumbent-party display fields in baselines come from
  current-day congress-legislators (assessments are unaffected — incumbency
  for ratings comes from the cycle's own FEC file).
- The GDELT discovery tier is excluded everywhere it matters
  (material=false) and from baselines entirely.
- State-court and county-level incidents (e.g. Cochise County 2022) are
  underrepresented in all cycles — CourtListener's state coverage is thin.
  A baseline miss there is a source limitation, not model vindication.

## Reproducing

PGURL=postgresql://…  RATINGS_FILE=/path/to/1976-2024-house.tab \
