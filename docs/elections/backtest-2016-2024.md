# Backtest: 2016–2024 baselines (methodology doc §43)

Rebuilt 2026-08-22 under methodology **2026.09.4** after the critical review
(`critical-review-2026-08-22.md`) found look-ahead bias in the original run.
This report replaces the earlier findings entirely; the superseded numbers
are kept at the bottom for the record.

## What changed and why the old headline was wrong

The original backtest read every prior cycle at zero high-risk pivotal races
and concluded 2026 was anomalous. That result was an artifact: replays
judged every event and case by its **current** lifecycle status, so a docket
that terminated in December contributed nothing on the August days it was
live. By election-day vantage, 93% of 2020's cases were terminated — so the
2020 replay saw almost no live litigation on any day. The live 2026 cycle,
whose cases have not had time to die, was being compared against baselines
with the litigation stripped out.

2026.09.4 records status-transition dates (docket termination, rule
supersession) and resolves each event's and case's status **as of the
replay day**. Residual unknown-transition rows are under 3% of cases per
cycle (0–1 events). The same rebuild applied the ruling-classifier fixes
(stays of injunctions no longer count as injunctions, filing-mechanics
orders are excluded, federal *plaintiffs* no longer nationalize a block,
identical orders on transferred dockets dedupe) — across the five baselines
that removed 74 misclassified entries and 48 duplicates.

## Headline series — high-risk pivotal races (intervention relevance ≥ .12, pivotality ≥ .1)

| | 2016 | 2018 | 2020 | 2022 | 2024 | 2026 full | 2026 matched |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| At ~73 days out | 0 | 0 | 56 | 64 | 80 | 98 | **80** |
| Final-100-day min/max | 0/0 | 0/0 | 27/60 | 52/84 | 41/103 | 81/98 (observed window) | — |
| At election day | 0 | 0 | 29 | 73 | 103 | — | — |
| Mean index, end of series | 1.5 | 1.2 | 3.6 | 4.8 | 6.0 | 6.0 | 5.0 |

**The finding, stated carefully:** on matched sources at the same distance
from election day, 2026 reads 80 — level with 2024 (80), above 2022 (64)
and 2020 (56), inside the recent-cycle range. 2024 finished at 103. **2026
is not anomalous.** It sits at the top of a range that litigious ordinary
cycles also occupy, and the model's reading is "as much live, pivotal-seat
election litigation as 2024 at this point," not a verdict that subversion
is underway.

## How to read each cycle

- **2016 / 2018 — zero throughout, but not from quiet dockets.** Both
  cycles carry saturated litigation exposure (.83/.82). The zeros come from
  the pivotality term: the margin-derived projections give 244R and 245R —
  blowout majorities — and national tightness clamps to 0, so no race is
  pivotal. This is the multiplicative design working as intended (a
  non-tight House can't flip on a handful of seats), not the model
  discriminating subversion risk. RECAP coverage is also thinner in these
  cycles; both effects point the same way.
- **2020 — high and declining.** 56 at 73 days out, decaying to 29 by
  election day as courts resolved cases (institutional resistance .55, the
  highest of any cycle — the "courts held" signal survives the correction,
  now in proportion). Note: 2020's projection allocates 434 of 435 seats,
  so tightness uses the 0.5 fallback.
- **2022 — high throughout** (52–84, ending 73). Redistricting-cycle
  litigation plus a genuinely tight projected House. 8 races lack a blended
  margin, so tightness also uses the 0.5 fallback.
- **2024 — the tightest cycle** (projection 218–217, tightness 1.0) with
  heavy litigation: 41 rising to 103 at election day.
- **2026 — 98 full model / 80 matched** at 73 days out, tightness .76.
  The full-model elevation over the matched number is the state-legislation
  layer (OpenStates), which cannot be replayed historically.

## Consequences

1. **The threat scale is calibrated to baseline percentiles** (owner
   decision, same day). `controlThreat` is now the live count's position
   in the pooled distribution of all 505 corrected baseline cycle-days
   (`packages/elections-shared/src/threat-calibration.ts`, regenerated by
   `baseline:export`): LOW below the median (51), MODERATE from the median,
   HIGH at or above the 90th percentile (84). The live full-model count
   (98) reads HIGH; the matched-source count (80) would read MODERATE —
   the level leans conservative because the live count includes the
   state-legislation source baselines cannot see (documented on the
   methodology page).
2. **The high-risk cutoff (.12) sits inside a dense part of the
   distribution** — day-to-day counts move by tens of races (2026 matched
   moved 58→80 across two replay days). Counts are best read as a band,
   not a point.

## Standing limitations

- Injunction-as-resistance is direction-blind: courts blocking expansions
  and restrictions both count as resistance (documented; visible in 2020).
- State-court and county incidents (Cochise-class) are under-covered in
  every cycle.
- Baselines' `stateCooperation` is structurally 0 (OpenStates cannot
  time-travel); comparisons use the matched-source 2026 run
  (`elections_sens_2026`).
- Members/incumbent display fields in baselines come from current-day
  congress-legislators; assessments are unaffected.

## Reproducing

```sh
PGURL=postgresql://…  RATINGS_FILE=/path/to/1976-2024-house.tab \
  scripts/elections/build-baseline.sh 2020        # any of 2016–2024
pnpm --filter @elections-tracker/worker reclassify:rulings   # per DB
pnpm --filter @elections-tracker/worker baseline:compare
pnpm --filter @elections-tracker/worker baseline:export
```

## Superseded results (2026.09.3, look-ahead biased — kept for the record)

The original run read: 2016–2022 zero for the entire final 100 days; 2024
zero until ~48 hours out (then 3); 2026 matched-sources 4. Its "no prior
cycle comes close" conclusion, and the "record activity met record
resistance and read zero" reading of 2020, were artifacts of judging
historical days by current case status.
